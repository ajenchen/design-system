# Upstream directives and SSOT mapping

## Integrity contract

The design-system audit skill owns the dimension inventory and detailed rubric. This workflow always consumes the complete current inventory and must not duplicate a mutable dimension count. A change to the audit rubric is made at its owner and inherited here through a path and digest.

The shared governance instruction, provider registry, skill-semantics registry, canonical rules, and release/fleet contracts remain their own owners. Generated provider views are projections, never alternate policy sources.

## Permanent objectives

The workflow must preserve these outcomes across local, cloud, source-repository, template, and consumer environments:

- complete rather than sampled review;
- equal evidence and judgment criteria for both providers;
- a genuinely independent second pass;
- explicit SSOT ownership and deterministic propagation;
- plain-language user decisions only for genuine product/UI/UX SSOT choices or tradeoffs;
- autonomous completion of engineering/governance policy, architecture, remediation, release,
  rollout, and other standing-delegated work whether substantive or not;
- reproducible verification, clean maintainable code, and no fabricated PASS;
- provider-specific mechanisms confined to registered adapter bindings;
- fail-closed onboarding for any future provider until compatibility is evidenced.

## Evidence mapping

For every objective, the final report identifies:

1. canonical source path and digest;
2. runtime/provider binding used;
3. coverage or verification artifact;
4. downstream generated views and consumers checked;
5. unresolved blocker or explicit incompatibility reason.

This mapping replaces historical provider-specific instructions and prevents the workflow from forking when a tool, model, transport, or repository surface changes.
