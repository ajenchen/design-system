# Relationships, anti-patterns, and review anchors

## Workflow composition

- Use the design-system audit skill as the sole owner of audit dimensions and detailed rubrics.
- Use the cross-provider collaboration workflow as the sole owner of independent-peer transport and brief equality.
- Use the proposal workflow for genuine product／UI／UX SSOT decisions retained by the user.
- Use the canonical-placement workflow for approved propagation and drift prevention.
- Use the governance-pruning workflow when the audit discovers duplicate, stale, or over-concrete rules.

This workflow composes those owners; it does not copy their mutable rules or counts.

## Permanent anti-patterns

- skipping canonical inputs because they appear unchanged;
- sampling, representative subsets, or unrecorded omissions;
- showing primary conclusions to the peer before its independent pass;
- treating a same-provider worker or renamed adapter as an independent peer;
- accepting a response without matching inventory/rubric digests;
- allowing reviewer mutation or failing to compare worktree fingerprints;
- accepting claims without reproducing their evidence;
- resolving disagreement by vote, authority label, or vague consensus;
- running repeated generative audits after verified material findings reach zero;
- claiming completion while a required gate is unavailable or unproven.

## External review anchors

Use current primary sources when comparing this protocol with mature review systems. Relevant patterns include independent academic peer review, maintainers plus public patch review, adversarial design review, separation of author and approver, immutable build provenance, and reproducible verification. These are comparison anchors, not substitute evidence for repository policy.
